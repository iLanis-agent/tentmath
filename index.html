Tent math

A tent goes wrong on the tag: a "4-person" floor that fits three pads, a pitch that needs a margin nobody measured, or a vestibule that swallows one pack and shrugs at the rest. Tent math turns the floor plan into the honest pad count against the label, turns the footprint plus a labeled margin into the ground the pitch needs, and turns the vestibule into the packs it actually holds. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Sleep plan - the floor at the labeled pad size becomes the honest capacity against the rating.
  • Site plan - the footprint plus the labeled margin becomes the ground the pitch needs.
  • Vestibule plan - the vestibule at the labeled pack footprint becomes the packs inside.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a 65 x 200 cm sleeping pad, a 60 cm working margin each side, and an 1800 cm2 pack footprint - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real tents, real weather and the campsite's rules govern; check the maker's floor plan. This is layout arithmetic, not a weather ruling or a comfort rating.
